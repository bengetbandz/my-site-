Red Flags on This Page

1Fake domain. The real registered domain is redelivery-check.example. The "ups.com" at the front is just a subdomain — UPS's actual site is ups.com, with nothing before it.
2QR code entry point. The victim scanned a "we missed you" door hanger. UPS delivery notices do not carry QR codes — they have a printed tracking number only.
3The "refundable $0.99" hook. A tiny, refundable amount is engineered to feel risk-free. UPS never charges a redelivery fee through a link, and never asks for card details to release a package.
4Phone + billing address + card details. This is the harvest. The "package" is the pretext; the phone number, address, and full card data are the prize.
5Urgency + authority. UPS branding, a delivery-exception status, and a "confirm now" CTA are all there to stop the victim from pausing to check the URL.

Why is there a fee?

To ensure secure and priority redelivery, UPS requires a small temporary holding fee of $0.99. This covers administrative handling and ensures your package is prioritized on the next delivery route.

This refundable fee is 100% refundable and will be credited back to your original payment method immediately after successful delivery.
